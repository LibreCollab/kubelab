PodDisruptionBudgets limit voluntary disruptions such as node drains. They do
not prevent hardware failure or normal rolling updates.

- `minAvailable` keeps a floor of ready Pods.
- `maxUnavailable` allows a bounded number to go down.
- The status shows current healthy Pods and allowed disruptions.

```bash
kubectl get pdb
kubectl describe pdb web
```

**Failure modes.** A PodDisruptionBudget does not prevent failures. It limits voluntary evictions and can block drains when too few Pods are ready. A PDB that selects no Pods or allows zero disruptions can make maintenance impossible.

**Verify.** Verify the selector, current healthy count, desired healthy count, and allowed disruptions. Compare them with the workload's replicas and readiness. Test a drain in a safe environment. A PDB is correct when it protects availability without deadlocking planned maintenance.

**Evidence to capture.** Record healthy replicas, desired healthy count, and allowed disruptions before a drain; those values explain most voluntary-eviction blocks.
